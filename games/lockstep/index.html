Lockstep

You do not control the drones. You write one program, and every drone on the board executes it at the same time, one instruction per beat. Getting them all onto the pad is not a matter of steering — it is a matter of finding a sequence that happens to work from every starting square at once.

The rules

  • One instruction per beat. Every drone obeys it.
  • A drone facing a wall does not move — and the instruction is still spent. That is how two drones that were out of step end up in the same square.
  • Drones pass through each other freely. They are not solid.
  • Collect every crystal before the drones gather, if the level has any. Any drone may take any crystal.
  • You win the moment all drones stand on the pad together, with nothing left to collect.

Why it works

Think of the board as a machine. Each instruction is a function that maps every square to another square — or back to itself, if a wall is in the way. Walls are the only thing that makes that map lose information, and losing information is exactly what you need: two drones can only end up in the same place if the map folded them together.

So the puzzle is to find a sequence of moves that collapses every starting square onto one. Mathematicians call that a synchronizing word. You will find it by shoving everything into a corner.
